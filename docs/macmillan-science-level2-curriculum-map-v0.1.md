## 1. Product decision and evidence boundary

Macmillan Science Level 2 is a **core subject in the AAS2 Grade 2 pathway**. It receives a first-class subject entry, curriculum map, digital book structure, lesson sequence, original question bank, skill-level assessment, progress tracking, and review scheduling. It is not an optional enrichment module.

This is a product curriculum decision. It does **not** claim that Science is listed as a compulsory subject in every Sudanese Grade 2 school or in the public Sudan Learning Passport catalogue.

The publisher's official Level 2 Scope and Sequence identifies 18 topics and their learning objectives. This map organises those published scope indicators into AAS2 strands and original learning outcomes. It does not reproduce workbook questions, teacher instructions, diagrams, illustrations, or page layouts.

| SCI-G2-ENQ-04 | Compare | Identifies similarities and differences using evidence | Correct comparison across two or more examples |

| SCI-G2-ENQ-10 | Communicate | Uses age-appropriate science vocabulary to describe a result | Clear oral, selected, or short written response |

## 5. Original question-authoring specification

Every production question must:
1. Map to one primary Skill ID and one lesson objective; optional secondary tags do not replace the primary mapping.
2. Test a science idea or practice, not merely recall an isolated word.
3. Use original AAS2 wording, examples, options, diagrams, narration, and illustrations.
4. Include one unambiguous keyed answer for single-choice items; distractors must reflect plausible misconceptions.
5. State the evidence expected and the misconception represented by each distractor.
6. Be suitable for a 7–8-year-old English learner: short sentences, controlled vocabulary, clear visual support, and no unnecessary reading load.
7. Separate science understanding from English spelling/grammar unless language itself is the learning objective.
8. Include safety review wherever an activity mentions heat, fire, food tasting, unknown plants, insects, liquids, or outdoor investigation.
9. Carry source-reference IDs and rights metadata. Macmillan is reference-only; do not reproduce its questions, activities, diagrams, photographs, teacher instructions, or page design.
10. Be reviewed for scientific accuracy, answer-key correctness, age suitability, accessibility, duplication, and asset integrity before release.

### Question and activity archetypes

- Identify / select an observation.
- Sort or classify by a visible rule.
- Match a plant/animal/organ/feature to a function.
- Sequence a growth or life-cycle process.
- Predict then compare with a result.
- Read a simple table, tally, or picture graph.
- Choose the safest action.
- Explain a simple cause/effect relationship.
- Apply a concept to a new familiar example.
- Design-and-improve challenge using safe household or classroom materials.

## 7. Suggested assessment and review model

- **Diagnostic:** 1–2 low-reading-load items to identify prerequisite understanding before a topic.
- **Guided practice:** immediate feedback after each attempt; do not count hint-assisted success as independent mastery evidence.
- **Independent check:** use a different representation or example from the teaching item.
- **Transfer check:** apply the same idea in a new but familiar context.
- **Delayed retrieval:** revisit the skill after a gap; store the date and result.
- **Mastery:** require consistent independent evidence across more than one item form and more than one session. Numeric thresholds remain provisional until item calibration.
- **Misconception response:** select a different representation or scaffold; do not merely repeat the same item.

## 8. Localisation and safety design

Use familiar Sudanese contexts when scientifically appropriate: beans, okra, dates, mangoes, local shade trees, garden plants, common domestic animals, local birds and insects, water containers, cooking safety, and night-sky observation. Local examples must not imply that every child has the same home resources or environment.

Practical activities must be safe and feasible offline. Do not ask children to taste unknown plants or substances, handle unknown insects, touch flames/hot objects, look directly at the Sun, or conduct unsupervised outdoor investigations. Activities involving heat, food, tools, or unknown materials require adult supervision and an explicit safe alternative.
