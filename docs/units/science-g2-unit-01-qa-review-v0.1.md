## 1. Executive QA finding

The unit's four-lesson structure and science explanations are broadly sound for an introductory Grade 2 treatment. The major defect was assessment construction, not the overall topic selection.

### Scientific review

- **L1:** Living/non-living distinction and observable growth are correct. Avoid defining life by movement or sound; plants are living even though they do not walk. Distinguish a living plant from wood or artificial replicas.
- **L2:** Roots, stems, leaves, edible plant parts and broccoli flower are suitable. State “usually” or “many plants” where plant structures/functions vary. A potato tuber is a modified stem, not a root.
- **L3:** Cotton, wood/paper, seeds and date palm uses are accurate. Use local examples without implying every paper product is made exclusively from wood or every cloth is cotton.
- **L4:** Germination is distinct from later seedling growth. For many familiar seeds, water, oxygen/air and suitable warmth support germination; light is important when the green seedling develops and makes food. Soil is not universally required for germination. Keep “damp, not flooded” and adult supervision.

### Assessment defects corrected in this revision

1. Key positions balanced: A = 10, B = 10, C = 12 (31.25%, 31.25%, 37.5%).
2. Distractors are plausible, age-appropriate misconceptions rather than jokes or unrelated objects.
3. Items test the named science concept, not unnecessary reading complexity.
4. Each item is tagged with skill, phase, difficulty and misconception.
5. Every option has an authoring rationale; incorrect feedback is concept-focused and non-revealing.
6. No item asks a child to taste an unknown plant or perform an unsupervised investigation.

## 5. Unit-level content edits recommended

1. Keep the existing four lesson structure and learning intentions.
2. Add the “usually / many plants” qualifiers where functions vary across plant species.
3. Retain the scientifically important note that germination can begin without soil and that light is needed for photosynthesis in green seedlings, not necessarily to start germination.
4. Ensure any illustrations label roots/stems/leaves accurately and do not show edible plant parts as universal across all plants.
5. Use Sudan-context examples (date palm, sorghum, sesame, cotton) as optional familiar contexts, not assumptions about every household.
6. Do not include Arabic helper text or a pre-question explanation in the current child activity. Reconsider only if a specific new learning need is evidenced and explicitly approved.
7. Do not encode these revised items in schema v3. Wait for the approved v4 content contract and supported renderer.
