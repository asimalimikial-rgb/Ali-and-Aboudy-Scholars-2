## Proposed observable outcomes

### Unit 3 — Senses and Our Bodies

- Match each sense to the organ that supports it and describe a simple everyday function.
- Identify sound sources and compare loud/quiet and high/low sounds without confusing pitch with loudness.
- Describe how eyes help us see and identify colours; use safe colour observation or mixing.
- Compare surfaces using tactile vocabulary such as smooth, rough, soft and hard.
- Make safe observations about taste and smell; distinguish observation from unsafe tasting of unknown substances.

### Unit 4 — Materials and Physical Properties

- Predict and test whether familiar objects float or sink; distinguish material properties from the effect of shape.
- Describe how liquids take the shape of a container and compare simple capacities.
- Identify materials that dissolve in water and distinguish dissolving from mixing of liquids such as oil and water.
- Identify common heat sources and safe/unsafe actions; know when to move away and ask an adult.

### Unit 5 — The Sky at Night

- Distinguish the Moon from stars and describe simple visible patterns.
- Recognise that the Moon has a surface with craters; use a safe model to investigate crater formation.
- Observe and describe a simple star pattern without claiming that a classroom model reproduces the scale of space.

## Activity design and assessment

Use varied evidence, not a sequence of recall-only multiple-choice items: observe and identify; sort/classify by an explicit rule; match organ, material or feature to a function; predict before a simple test; sequence an observation or process; read a small tally/picture record; select a safe action; explain a result using one visible or recorded observation.

For every activity, record one primary skill, observable outcome, prompt language, correct response or rubric, misconception/distractor rationale, difficulty, assessment phase, media requirements, and source references. Include diagnostic, guided practice, independent check, transfer, and delayed retrieval evidence where appropriate. There is no fixed question-count ceiling; author enough varied evidence to cover the outcomes without repetition or filler.

## Safety and localisation

- Never ask children to look directly at the Sun.
- Do not expose children to flames, hot objects, boiling water, electrical equipment or unsafe heating experiments. Use illustrations or safe adult-demonstrated models.
- Never ask children to taste unknown plants, liquids, powders or foods. Any food example must allow a no-taste alternative and account for allergy/safety concerns.
- Keep sound demonstrations at safe volume.
- Do not ask children to touch unknown substances or insects.
- Use familiar Sudanese examples when scientifically appropriate, but do not assume every child has the same household materials, environment or access to equipment.

## Completion gates

A unit is not complete until each lesson has an explicit learning intention and observable outcome; original explanations, examples, visual models and activities are authored; scientific accuracy and age suitability are reviewed independently; English reading load is appropriate; answer keys and distractor rationales are checked; safety and accessibility are reviewed; media and offline fallbacks validate; Content Validation and Android CI pass; and the unit hierarchy is integrated in runtime and tested on Android.

No Units 3–5 activities are counted as authored or integrated by this planning document.
